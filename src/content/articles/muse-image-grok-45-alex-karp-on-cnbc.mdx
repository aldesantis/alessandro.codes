## Summary

Meta launched Muse Image, a new AI tool that helps users and advertisers create better images on its apps. SpaceXAI released Grok 4.5, an AI focused on finance, legal, and coding, emphasizing control over data and models. Alex Karp stressed that companies want to own their AI data and models to protect their business value and avoid giving too much to AI labs.

## Highlights

- One of the single most important contributors to model improvement is verifiability. A major reason why coding has been such a fruitful area of AI development is because code is verifiable: does it work or not? That, by extension, means that the more that a particular task is verifiable, the faster it will be done by AI. Or, to put it another way, owning verification is extremely advantageous, and markets are verification mechanisms. Yes, you need to have enough liquidity to get sufficient signal — there is a _lot_ of noise in this data — but that is exactly what Meta ads are: arguably the most liquid market in the world, with trillions of ads driving conversions or not. Any model that plugs directly into that signal is going to get better sooner.
- To take this full circle, this is another reason to be a bit bullish about Meta: they operate in markets where it is a given that Meta gets and acts on all of the data; they don’t need to fight enterprise data policies. Anthropic, meanwhile, appears to be making a bet that they can be better enough that they can get the data they need anyways (and that said data will drive that virtuous cycle where their offering improves that much more rapidly); they might cash it in.
