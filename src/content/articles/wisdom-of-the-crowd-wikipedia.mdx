## Summary

The wisdom of the crowd means that a group’s combined answers are often better than any single person’s. It works best when people make independent guesses and when there is a clear correct answer. However, crowd decisions can be less accurate if people influence each other or if the question has no exact solution.

## Highlights

- **“Wisdom of the crowd”** or “**wisdom of the majority”** expresses the notion that the collective opinion of a diverse and independent group of individuals (rather than that of a single expert) yields the best [judgement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judgement).[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisdom_of_the_crowd/#cite_note-1) This concept, while not new to the [Information Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Age), has been pushed into the spotlight by social information sites such as [Quora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quora), [Reddit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reddit), [Stack Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stack_Exchange), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia), [Yahoo! Answers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahoo!_Answers), and other web resources which rely on collective human knowledge.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisdom_of_the_crowd/#cite_note-2) An explanation for this supposition is that the idiosyncratic noise associated with each individual judgment is replaced by an average of that noise taken over a large number of responses, tempering the effect of the noise.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisdom_of_the_crowd/#cite_note-WoC-Combi-3)
- This has contributed to the insight in cognitive science that a crowd’s individual judgments can be modeled as a [probability distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_distribution) of responses with the median centered near the true value of the quantity to be estimated.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisdom_of_the_crowd/#cite_note-Surowiecki-8)
