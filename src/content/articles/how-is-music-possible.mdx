## Summary

Music can create powerful emotions and a sense of connection, though no single theory fully explains how. Its simple notes give rise to an enormous range of experiences. The author finds this mystery astonishing and hopes we will explore more kinds of music.

## Highlights

- For Schopenhauer, the world that we perceive—all the objects and events—is the outward manifestation of an underlying reality he called _Will_. And music, he thought, is unique among the arts because unlike painting and narrative, which depict the trees and people and situations that the Will manifests itself as, music depicts the Will directly; the other arts “speak only of shadows, but [music] speaks of the thing itself.” Since humans are also manifestations of the Will—we experience its force as desire and frustration and effort and so on—we recognize our deepest nature in the emotions evoked by music because… music is a representation of the same thing as us.
- I wonder if music is perhaps best thought of as an emergent phenomenon. It is built from small, mundane actions (plucking a string, manipulating our emotional processing, synchronizing our body movements with others to create a sense of bonding, and so on). But the thing that emerges (these swooping existential experiences) can’t be understood by just looking at the building blocks—much as the behavior of a sand dune can’t be understood by considering a single grain of sand. Sand dunes move around, larger ones more slowly than smaller ones, and when they collide, they exchange sand in various ways—none of which are properties that sand grains have.
