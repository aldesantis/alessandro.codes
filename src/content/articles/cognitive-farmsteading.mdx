## Summary

AI is changing how we think by making it possible to work and create ideas in small, personal settings instead of big cities. This new way, called cognitive farmsteading, mixes technology with individual creativity at home. As a result, people can explore unique ideas without needing large groups, leading to more diverse and homemade intellectual work.

## Highlights

- The urban intellectual life, however, has always come at a price.
  You trade some originality and independence of thought for the leverage and competitive/collaborative social energy of your chosen milieu. Those without the strength of mind to resist groupthink, status competition, and [the tyrannies of shared subcultural tastes](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/the-taste-essay), either become hollowed out intellectual automatons, or retreat periodically to [waldenpond](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/against-waldenponding) and restore their battered individuality.
  On the other side of the equation, rural and small-town intellectual culture, such as it has been, has historically exhibited a certain modest capacity for generating outlier intellectual originality and weirdness, and in specialized domains (such as farming and animal husbandry of course, but also other fields that benefit from geographic scale and dispersion), steady innovation.
- In isolation, the human brain rapidly loses the ability to adequately challenge and surprise itself. And until recently, the only type of matter in the universe capable of challenging and strength-training the human brain was other human brains. A mountain may challenge a mountain climber in specialized ways, but until 2026, only another human mind could truly challenge a human mind in general ways.
- My claim is, _AI enables a powerful mode of intellectual rural domesticity_ — the ability to be highly intellectually productive in “rural” modes, a historically unprecedented condition. And one reason I’ve taken so readily and eagerly to AI is that I already lean that way.
- AI-detection tooling is important when deepfakes are used for deliberate fraud or disinformation, but as an inquisitorial instrument of moral policing, it creates a kind of morality theater at best. As weaponry for witch-hunting, it seems mostly harmless.
- I spent two decades writing in public with first-to-insight ideas as my stock-in-trade. Being early to spot something happening, and giving it a roughly right characterization and evocative name, was basically what I did across hundreds of blog posts and newsletters. And it worked because lots of other people were on my heels in each case, and lots _more_ people had unconsciously recognized, but not named or characterized, the thing I was point out. Being consistently just _slightly_ ahead of a mass of properly primed subconscious collective attention is valuable, and can pay the bills if you do it right.
- Today, chances are, for anything worth thinking about, everybody capable of thinking about it is already doing so with AI in the loop, developing similar understandings, down to similar metaphors and neologisms. While useful to the individual, it does make it harder and more pointless, to talk to other humans about your thinking in the usual ways.
- _AI is a domestic technology._ We use it in ways that reflect the idiosyncratic needs of n=1-4 users, the way we use various technologies and services to customize homes.
  As a technology of _intellectual_ rural domesticity, AI drives us to heavily customize the ideas we consume and produce for our own needs, with a great deal of DIYery.
