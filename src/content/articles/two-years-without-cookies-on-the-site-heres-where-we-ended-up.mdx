## Summary

Sentry removed all advertising cookies from their site two years ago and changed how they spend their growth budget. They now focus on building brand awareness through authentic channels like podcasts, YouTube, and partnerships instead of relying on measurable ads. This shift helped them grow users and understand customers better despite losing detailed tracking data.

## Highlights

- Research from the [IPA](https://business.linkedin.com/marketing-solutions/content-marketing/b2b-trends/the-war-on-brand) (popularized by LinkedIn’s B2B Institute) suggests the optimal split between brand building and direct-response is around 60/40 in favor of brand. But most companies do the opposite; [Refine Labs found](https://dreamdata.io/blog/proven-b2b-growth-strategies-from-the-brand-demand-expand-framework) that the typical company puts roughly 80% of budget into things with immediate measurable results (paid search, retargeting) and only 20% into things that build long-term awareness. The reason is simple: it’s way easier to justify spending money when you can point to a dashboard that says “we spent X and got Y signups.”
- I like to take a weekly time series of our signups by channel and just annotate which weeks we do major things. It’s not perfect attribution. But it’s directional, and directional is enough to keep stacking good decisions while ditching experiments that don’t land. I don’t want to come off as flippant, but sometimes I think we overcomplicate this. Tracking awareness can be simpler than people expect.
