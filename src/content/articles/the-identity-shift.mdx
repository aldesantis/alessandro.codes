## Summary

AI agents are becoming gatekeepers between customers and brands, shaping what people discover and buy. Brands must make information easy for agents to use while still creating meaningful human connections. Success will depend on trustworthy business practices and adapting to who controls customer data.

## Highlights

- I said the agent works for the customer, not the brand. And in truth, it works for whoever owns it. When the owner earns a percentage of the basket, Jenny’s interests and the platform’s interests start to drift apart, and she has no way of knowing which one is doing the choosing.
- Identity as a Product: Brands serve as self-expression tools, with purchases reflecting personal identity (e.g., owning a Mac or wearing Patagonia).
  - Identity as Targeting: Marketers use identity traits to segment audiences and deliver tailored messages, fostering communities around shared values (e.g., Nike runners, outdoor enthusiasts).
    - Note: I would flip this and talk about the function of brands instead. Brands serve two purposes: one is to express identity, the other is to overcome information asymmetry. Agents will take over the latter but by definition they cannot take over the former.
- Most brands provide enough structured data for basic e-commerce, but not the depth and real-time accuracy needed for sophisticated agent decision-making.
    - Note: Is this actually true? Everyone says agents need machine readable data, but it seems like the main strength of LLMs is translating between the domains of humans and machines. Machine readable data helps, but sometimes I suspect we obsess a bit too much on making everything completely readable by machines.
- Consumer Sovereignty: Privacy primitives and zero-knowledge proofs enable consumers to maintain control over their behavioral data, choosing what to share and with whom. Businesses must negotiate access to their own customer insights.
    - Note: I am not really sure this will be the case. We have been trying to sopve for data sovereignty for years if not decades. What makes us think AI will finally bring it about? If anything, I expect the convenience afforded by LLMs will be more than worth the abdication on data sovereignty.
- The gap between what you say about your business and what you actually do? That gap is now visible. Measurable. And factored into every decision agents make on behalf of customers.
    - Note: This still needs to be proven out. So far gents seem to be pretty gullible, e.g., trusting a brand’s own claims that it makes from its first party website. Perhaps we will get there, but not yet. Also this doesn’t account for the manipulation that will become possible through ads.
- Jenny still makes choices. She still has agency. But the menu of options, the context for decisions, the suggestions that feel like they came from nowhere—those are increasingly curated by algorithms optimising for outcomes she never explicitly chose.
    - Note: And right now they are not? It’s just different algorithms, perhaps even more opaque ones.
