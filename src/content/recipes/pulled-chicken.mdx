## Steps

1. In a bowl, combine the chicken thighs, beer, paprika, garlic powder, and barbecue sauce. Cover and marinate in the fridge for at least one hour.
2. Slice the onions. In a heavy, wide pot or Dutch oven with a lid, melt the butter over medium-low heat, add the onions and brown sugar, and cook, stirring now and then, for ten to fifteen minutes, until soft and lightly golden.
3. Lift the chicken out of the marinade (keep the liquid) and pat it dry. Push the onions to the side, raise the heat to medium-high, and brown the chicken for two to three minutes per side. It does not need to cook through.
4. Pour in the reserved marinade, scraping up anything stuck to the bottom, then stir in the tomato paste, salt, and pepper. The liquid should come about halfway up the chicken.
5. Bring to a boil, then turn the heat to its lowest setting, cover, and braise at a bare simmer—just an occasional bubble—for sixty to seventy-five minutes, turning the chicken once halfway through. It is ready when it pulls apart easily with a fork; thighs are safe from 74°C (165°F) but only turn tender at around 90°C (195°F) inside.
6. Transfer the chicken to a board and let it rest for about ten minutes. Meanwhile, raise the heat to medium and reduce the cooking liquid uncovered for ten to fifteen minutes, until thick and glossy.
7. Shred the chicken with two forks, return it to the pot, and toss it in the reduced sauce until coated.
