## Steps

1. The night before, put the chocolate chips in the freezer.
2. Make the tangzhong: whisk the flour into the cold milk in a small saucepan, then cook over low heat, stirring constantly, until it thickens into a glossy paste (about 65°C). Cover with plastic wrap touching the surface and let it cool completely.
3. In the bowl of a stand mixer fitted with the dough hook, crumble the yeast into the milk, add the honey, and stir to dissolve.
4. Add the flour, the cooled tangzhong, the egg, and the vanilla, and knead on low speed until no dry flour remains.
5. Add the sugar in two additions, waiting for the first to be absorbed before adding the second.
6. Knead on medium speed for 10–15 minutes, stopping now and then to turn the dough over, until it is smooth and elastic and pulls away from the sides of the bowl.
7. Add the butter a few cubes at a time, letting each addition disappear before adding the next, then add the salt.
8. Keep kneading until the dough is glossy and stretches into a thin, translucent sheet without tearing. Let it rest for 5 minutes.
9. Add the frozen chocolate chips and mix on the lowest speed for 1–2 minutes, just until they are evenly distributed.
10. Turn the dough out onto a lightly greased surface, fold the edges toward the center a few times, and shape it into a tight ball.
11. Place the dough in a lightly greased bowl, cover, and let it rise in the oven with only the light on until doubled, 2–3 hours.
12. Divide the dough into sixteen pieces of about 70 grams each, weighing them, and keep them covered.
13. Flatten each piece, fold the edges underneath, and roll it under a cupped palm into a smooth, tight ball.
14. Arrange the buns well apart on two baking sheets lined with parchment paper, cover with a clean cloth, and let them rise until puffy and nearly doubled, 1–1½ hours.
15. Heat the oven to 180°C (conventional, no fan).
16. Beat the egg yolk with the milk and brush the buns gently.
17. Bake one sheet at a time for 14–16 minutes, until deep golden.
18. Transfer the buns to a wire rack, cover with a cloth, and let them cool completely before storing.

## Notes

- The tangzhong replaces the yeasted starter most recipes use: the precooked flour holds on to more water, so the buns stay soft for days instead of drying out overnight.
- Frozen chips are what keeps the crumb pale. Never proof the dough anywhere warmer than an oven with the light on, or the chocolate will melt and streak.
- Mix as little as possible once the chips are in.
- The dough is soft and slightly sticky. Resist adding flour—at most a spoonful if it will not come together after long kneading.
- For a flavor closer to the packaged original, replace the butter with 50 grams of sunflower oil, added in a thin stream with the salt.
- Add the grated zest of half an orange with the vanilla for a more aromatic dough.
- For smaller buns, divide into twenty-three pieces of about 50 grams and bake for 12–13 minutes.
- If the buns brown too quickly, lower the oven to 170°C and bake a minute or two longer.
- With instant dry yeast, use 4 grams and mix it straight into the flour.
- Store in a sealed food bag at room temperature for three to four days, bagging them only once they are completely cool. They freeze well for up to two months; thaw at room temperature and warm briefly in a low oven.
