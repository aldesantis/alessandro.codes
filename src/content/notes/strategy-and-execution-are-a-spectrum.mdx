Strategy and execution aren’t a binary. Every piece of work sits somewhere on a spectrum, depending on how much of the intent is still open when it reaches you.

In software, the spectrum runs through typing out a function someone else specified, implementing a feature from a spec, designing the system behind it, choosing which features to build, and choosing which customers to build for. Each step involves less translation and more meaningmaking (see [[execution-is-translation-strategy-is-meaningmaking|execution is translation, strategy is meaningmaking]]).

Almost no work is pure translation, since even typing out a specified function involves small judgment calls. But the more of the intent is already settled, the less meaningmaking is left to do.

Very few jobs sit at a single point on the spectrum. Most people do a mix, and the mix is what matters, because [[ai-absorbs-work-in-a-predictable-order|AI absorbs work]] starting from the execution end.
