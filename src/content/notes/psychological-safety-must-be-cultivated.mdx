It’s interesting to note that psychological safety is NOT natural: our instinct is to protect ourselves by not speaking up.

The reason is that the pros of speaking up are usually delayed (_something might go wrong if you do not speak up_), while the cons are usually immediate (_your boss will certainly berate you for speaking up_).

Therefore, you can’t expect people to speak up out of sheer willpower, because it’s unnatural. The trick to fostering psychological safety, then, is to completely flip the equation:

- You want to make the pros more immediate. This is typically done by systematizing psychological safety at the organization level (and, most importantly, [[psychological-safety-is-a-team-factor|at the team level]]) and [[we-get-what-we-reward-for|rewarding people]] for speaking up rather than punishing them.
- You want to punish those who _don’t_ speak up by framing it as a disservice to the user/customer. If someone has an opinion or critical thought, they should feel obligated to share it with the group.
