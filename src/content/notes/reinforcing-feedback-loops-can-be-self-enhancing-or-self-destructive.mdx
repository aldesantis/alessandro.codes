Through reinforcing feedback loops, a system effectively causes its own behavior, ending up in a virtuous or vicious cycle depending on whether the behavior is desirable. Over time, this can lead to exponential improvements or total system collapse (usually by [[renewable-resources-are-flow-limited|pushing a stock past the point of no return]]).

Unfortunately, loops are hard to spot from the inside, because any single arc looks like plain cause and effect. Seeing the loop means [[systems-must-be-inspected|inspecting the system]] and tracing a consequence back around to its own cause, which is why you have to [[focus-on-second-order-outcomes|think in second-order terms]] to notice one at all.

All of this makes a vicious cycle a tempting target for brute force. Resist that. Since [[systems-strive-for-self-preservation|systems strive for self-preservation]], the loop will treat you as a foreign body, and [[swimming-downstream-is-easier-than-swimming-upstream|swimming downstream]] (i.e., redirecting the loop’s own momentum) beats trying to break it from the outside.
