## Highlights

- Sometimes the best credibility comes from listening extremely hard and then speaking.
- Unfortunately, younger SEs can sometimes be too blunt – directly saying, “that is never going to work!” to your client may not be the best approach.
- If you do not know the answer to a question, say so, and then promise to get it for the customer. Do not guess! You can only do this a few times in a meeting – excessive “don’t knows” shows that someone is in the wrong meeting. Sometimes it’s you; sometimes it is the customer.
- First, you need to establish control over your time (calendar) and inbox, as it is far more likely that a lack of time, rather than a lack of desire or talent, will hurt your reliability.
- Reliability at its best isn’t just about doing what you say; it is also about anticipating what the customer may need and preparing for that eventuality.
- Don’t forget the personal side of things. Be emotionally consistent and professionally levelheaded. Note that this does not mean emotionally distanced and detached, but that you shouldn’t be doom-and-gloom one day and rainbows and puppy dog happy the next.
- the #1 thing most mid to senior-level executives expect from a vendor’s sales engineering organization is someone who understands their business.
- Don’t fall victim to Premature Solutioneering. Even if you know you can help and fix the pain, you must let the client finish talking. Coming up with a solution too quickly can hurt your Credibility, at least until the client has total faith in you. Customers often feel they are unique but secretly hope they are not.
